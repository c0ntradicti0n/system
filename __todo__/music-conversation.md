2. Dynamics: The Systematic Unfolding

When multiple notes sound together, they are not just a harmonic interval; they have a dynamic ratio.

Fundamental (loudest)

Octave (quieter)

Fifth (quieter still)

Fourth (even quieter)

Major third, minor seventh, etc...

The difference between a pitch and a chord is not qualitative in kind but a matter of how the dynamics of overtones are distributed and perceived. A trombone playing a low C with specific dynamic, and flutes and strings playing the overtones at exactly the simulated dynamic ratios — it blurs the line between timbre and harmony.

This is the Summation/Kinetics of dynamics:

One note *p*, then another *p*, then another *p*... (uniform, the "crystal").

One note louder than the previous: an accent chain.

A gradual increase: crescendo.

A gradual decrease: diminuendo.

Sudden changes: terrace dynamics (baroque echo effects).

This is the bare positing of dynamic events in time. Like rhythm without meter, pure dynamic succession is mere difference. But the ear groups them: a crescendo is heard as a single gesture, a vector. An accent on a specific beat within a meter creates a dynamic grid that interacts with the rhythmic grid.
